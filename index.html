Cerenome, Inc. · Nasdaq: CNSY · Houston, Texas · formerly Plus Therapeutics

Cerenome says it is building an integrated CNS oncology platform connecting diagnostics, therapeutics, and clinical data into one system.

The 10-K, filed under the former name Plus Therapeutics, describes the company as “a U.S. healthcare company developing and commercializing precision diagnostics and targeted radiopharmaceuticals for central nervous system (‘CNS’) cancers.” Company statement 10-K

The company changed its name from Plus Therapeutics, Inc. to Cerenome, Inc. effective August 3, 2026; its Nasdaq symbol changed to CNSY from PSTV the same day, and the CUSIP did not change. Filed 8-K, Aug 4 8-K, Apr 2 10-Q

Read first: four things the filings say

  1. Going concern. The 10-Q says the company “has had, and will continue to have, an ongoing need to raise additional cash from outside sources” and that “These factors raise substantial doubt about the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern.” Company statement 10-Q The same doubt is stated in the two earlier 10-Qs, the 10-K and the 10-K’s auditor’s report, which carries an explanatory paragraph on going concern; all of them predate the September 2026 financing. At June 30, 2026 the company held cash and cash equivalents of $2,366,000 and investments of $6,221,000. Filed 10-Q, May 10-Q, Oct 2025 10-K
  2. Funding. After the quarter, on September 4, 2026, the company agreed to sell senior secured convertible notes of up to $21,276,596 in original principal to one institutional investor, secured by substantially all of its assets, with $3,000,000 of gross proceeds expected at the first closing. A press release furnished with the 8-K says the company “received initial funding at closing,” and the 8-K does not state the amount received. Cash used in operating activities in the six months to June 30, 2026 was $13,370,000. Filed 8-K, Sep 10 10-Q
  3. Listing. After a 1-for-25 reverse split effective April 2, 2026, Nasdaq notified the company on April 20, 2026 that it had regained compliance with the minimum bid price requirement. The 10-Q says the company is in compliance with the minimum stockholders’ equity requirement and is subject to a panel monitoring period through August 22, 2026, during which a new shortfall would bring a delist determination with no compliance plan or extra time; stockholders’ equity was $5,463,000 at June 30. The earlier notices and hearings are listed under funding, and no filing used here after the 10-Q reports a Nasdaq notice. Filed 8-K, Apr 2 8-K, Apr 21 10-Q
  4. Grant revenue. All reported revenue is grant revenue: $1,439,000 in the six months to June 30, 2026 against $2,449,000 a year earlier. The 10-Q says the company “started commercial billing for diagnostic revenue” but the estimated net revenue was “immaterial for recognition in the statement of operations.” The 10-Q says the CPRIT contract “will terminate on August 28, 2026” unless ended earlier; the company had received about $15.9 million of a grant of up to $17.6 million. Platform

Diagnostics, therapeutics and data, described as one system

The company’s website says “Cerenome connects precision diagnostics, targeted therapeutics, and patient data into an integrated central nervous system (CNS) oncology platform designed to improve how CNS cancers are detected, treated, and understood.” The filings describe the products one by one; the stage labels below are the website’s own. Company site Home page

  1. 01 Diagnostics: CNSide®

    The website says CNSide “detects, characterizes, and monitors CNS cancer from cerebrospinal fluid.” The 10-K says the company re-introduced the CNSide Cerebrospinal Fluid Tumor Cell Enumeration test, a laboratory developed test, in August 2025, and that its Houston laboratory has a CMS certificate of accreditation under CLIA. The 10-Q lists payer coverage and reimbursement for the assay among its risks. Company statement 10-K 10-Q Company site Home page

  2. 02 Therapeutics: REYOBIQ™

    The website calls REYOBIQ the lead of “a portfolio of targeted radiotherapeutics being designed to deliver where conventional therapies often cannot.” The 10-Q gives its chemical name: “rhenium (186 Re) obisbemeda (REYOBIQ).” Company statement 10-Q Company site Home page

  3. 03 Data insights

    The website says “Clinical data and AI help to generate insights that improve future patient care,” and labels its Clinical Intelligence Platform “Launching soon.” Company site Home page

The 10-K describes ReSPECT-GBM as a Phase 1/2 multicenter, open-label, volume and dose escalation study of the safety, tolerability and distribution of rhenium (186 Re) obisbemeda given by CED catheters. It says the drug has orphan drug designation for GBM and Fast Track designation for recurrent GBM, and that a $3.0 million award from the U.S. National Institutes of Health/National Cancer Institute “is expected to provide financial support” through a Phase 2 trial “including enrollment of up to 55 patients.” Company statement 10-K

Leptomeningeal metastasesREYOBIQ, ReSPECT-LM
Phase 2

The 10-K says that in December 2025 the company reported completion of the ReSPECT-LM single-dose trial: well tolerated up to a maximum tolerated dose of 66mCi, with a recommended phase 2 dose of 44.1 mCi. It adds that the ReSPECT-LM multi-dose Phase 1/2 study “is currently enrolling patients for the Phase 2 portion of the Study.” The 10-Q says CPRIT funds development of the drug for this indication. The 10-K and 10-Q add that at the November 2025 end-of-phase-1 meeting the “FDA conveyed that while accelerated approval may be appropriate for this indication, there are insufficient data to support the use of CTCs as an intermediate clinical endpoint,” and recommended an endpoint with established clinical benefit, such as overall survival. The September notes, term by term

The 8-K says each note is issued with an original issue discount of 6.0%, accrues interest at 8.0% a year (up to 18% after an event of default) and matures one year after issue. The notes are secured by a first-priority security interest in substantially all assets of the company and CNSide Diagnostics, LLC, including a pledge of the equity of CNSide. The initial conversion price of the initial notes is $2.74 a share with a floor price of $0.50; on an event of default the holder can convert at the lowest of the conversion price, 85% of the prior-day VWAP and a lower VWAP-based price. A change of control lets the holder require redemption in cash at a price that includes a 120% redemption premium. The holder cannot own more than 4.99% of the shares (up to 9.99% at its election), and each note has an exchange cap intended to comply with Nasdaq rules, subject to stockholder approval or an opinion of counsel. Interest is guaranteed through maturity even if a note is converted or redeemed earlier. CNSide is to pay the investor each quarter 2.5% of its gross revenues, subject to customary exclusions and to a quarterly cap of 2.5% of the unpaid balance of the notes, within 30 days after quarter end, until the notes are paid in full; the company guarantees it. The second notes are issued when the resale registration statement is effective, and the investor can elect, and in some conditions the company can require, purchases of the additional notes for 18 months after the initial closing. The company intends to use net proceeds for working capital and general corporate purposes. Filed 8-K, Sep 10

The 8-K body describes the initial closing as expected on or about September 10, 2026 and does not state the amount received. A press release furnished as Exhibit 99.1 to the same 8-K, dated September 10, calls the deal “a senior secured facility of up to $20 million” with 3i, LP, says the company “received initial funding at closing,” and says: “We forecast our cash runway is now well into 2028 when we consider, the 3i facility, other existing facilities along with current cash and forecasted CNSide related sales and cash flow.” Company statement 8-K, Sep 10

The CPRIT grant is up to $17.6M, of which about $15.9M had been received at June 30, 2026. It carries a matching-fund requirement (one dollar for every two dollars awarded), and revenue-sharing and royalty obligations on commercialization of rhenium (186 Re) obisbemeda until CPRIT receives 400% of the proceeds awarded. The contract “will terminate on August 28, 2026” unless ended earlier. CPRIT may require repayment of disbursed funds, with interest not above 5% a year, if CPRIT ends the contract for the company’s default, the company ends it for convenience, or the company moves its principal place of business out of Texas; the grant receivable was $1.8M at June 30, 2026 against $0.3M at December 31, 2025. The 10-Q also describes a $3.0 million Department of Defense award over three years from September 1, 2024. Filed 10-Q
